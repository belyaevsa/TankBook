## What to build

### 1. The screen, per the artboard
Two sections – deleted entries, and **"Overwritten by sync"** – plus the "Delete all now" footer.

Each row shows what the entry was (so it is recognisable), when it was deleted, and **how many days
remain**. The countdown is the point: it tells the user how long they have to change their mind.

### 2. Restore
Restoring clears the tombstone and the entry returns to the Log **and to the statistics** – hard rule 2
means a full-vehicle recompute follows, exactly as an edit does (`SCHEMA.md` → Recalculation on edit
lists restore explicitly). A restored entry that does not move the headline back is a bug.

### 3. The 30-day purge
Tombstones older than the grace period are purged locally. The repository already has a purge honouring
the grace period – **wire it to run**, do not reimplement it. Decide and state where it runs (app launch,
background, or both) and make it **idempotent** – running twice must not be different from running once.

**"Delete all now"** purges immediately, and it is destructive: a **system confirmation dialog**, which
per hard rule 5 is the one place red is allowed.

### 4. The "Overwritten by sync" section
From `docs/SYNC.md` S1/S4: when a merge replaces the local version, the losing version is **kept, not
discarded** – that is the local undo log the S-scenarios refer to. Show it here with **Compare**.

Sync does not exist until P4, so this section is **fixture-driven** – seed it through a launch argument
and say so in your report. Build the presentation and the restore path; the real merge feeds it later.

### 5. Empty state
No deleted entries is the normal case. Say so plainly and reassuringly – this screen existing at all is
the reassurance. No "N/A", no fabricated rows.

## Tests

**L1 (`ios/Tests/TankbookCoreTests/`, macOS, no simulator)**:
- **Restore returns the entry to statistics**: delete a fill-up, the headline changes; restore it, the
  headline returns to **exactly** its prior value. This is hard rule 8 made executable.
- **Purge honours the grace period**: a tombstone at 29 days survives, at 31 days it is purged. Assert
  both sides of the boundary, not just one.
- **Purge is idempotent**: running it twice equals running it once.
- **Purged entries are gone from the backup export too** – a purge is a real deletion, not a hide.
- **Days-remaining arithmetic**: 30 days from deletion, counting down, never negative; an entry deleted
