## The idea, and why it is not "guess a digit"

On a **pump display**, when `liters x unitPrice` misses `total` by roughly one least-significant
step of one operand, the likely cause is **one misread segment**, not three independent errors -
seven-segment glyphs differ by a single lit bar. Two fixtures show it:

- `pump-015`: reads `1.884` under glare, where `15.89 x 1.889 = 30.02` exactly.
- `pump-013`: the same correction resolves `7.34 x 1.779 = 13.06`.

Confusable pairs on seven segments: **4/9, 8/9, 8/6, 8/0, 3/9, 5/6, 1/7**.

## The rule that keeps this honest

**Accept a repair only when EXACTLY ONE substitution closes the arithmetic.** If two different
substitutions both close it, the document does not determine the answer and the parser **returns
nil** - hard rule 13, a value the app cannot know is left for the user. Inventing a digit is worse
than abstaining, and the corpus already paid for that lesson: `docs/EXTRACTION.md` records that
Vision returned a **wrong digit at confidence 1.00** on `pump-004`, and that the cross-check is
blind to swaps and scale shifts.

**Pump source only.** Thermal print has no segment topology, so a receipt is never repaired. A
repair on a receipt would be a fabricated number with no physical story behind it.

**A repair is a SUGGESTION, not a lock.** Return it with confidence lowered, never as a confirmed
field (hard rule 13, and `docs/EXTRACTION.md` -> the confirm sheet dims an unconfirmed value).
