**This is the QR-PARSER HALF ONLY**, and its purpose was deliberately narrowed on

**Read this framing before anything else, because it decides what you build.** The QR was
originally sold as "scan it and the fill-up is complete, 100% accurate, no OCR". That is
**false and cannot be made true**: the payload carries total, timestamp and three fiscal ids
and *nothing else* - no litres, no unit price, no fuel kind - and the OFD lookup that would
supply them is keyed on an opaque id **not derivable from the QR**, verified against two
different OFDs. Only **9 of 16** corpus receipts even carry a decodable QR.

So the QR is **not a capture path**. It is an **authoritative anchor for the OCR result**, and
that is worth a great deal, because picking the wrong total is the parser's worst failure. On
the real corpus the QR's `s` fixes three of the parser's four total errors outright (it read
VAT twice and a rounding line once), and on the fourth - a mixed fuel+water receipt - the QR's
grand total *disagreeing* with the fuel line is exactly how mixedness is detected.

Build for that job. Enrichment is **permanently deferred**, not pending.

## What to build

### 1. `FiscalQRPayload` + parser, in `ios/Sources/TankbookCore/Fiscal/`

```swift
public struct FiscalQRPayload: Equatable, Sendable {
    public let timestamp: Date
    public let total: Decimal
    public let fiscalDriveNumber: String    // fn
    public let documentNumber: String       // i
    public let fiscalSign: String           // fp
    public let operationType: Int           // n
}
```

- Parse from `String`. Order-independent (do not assume `t` comes first), tolerant of extra
  unknown keys (the format may grow), case-insensitive on keys.
- **`total` must be `Decimal`, parsed from the string, never `Double`.** `s=4201.68` through a
  binary float is a money bug; `docs/SCHEMA.md` is explicit that money is decimal.
- Timestamps are **local time, no zone in the payload**. Parse with an explicit calendar and an
  injected time zone; do not let the device's zone silently change the value. Support both
  `yyyyMMdd'T'HHmm` and `yyyyMMdd'T'HHmmss`.
- Return a typed error for malformed input; never `fatalError`, never force-unwrap.

### 2. The anchor: what the QR authoritatively knows

A value describing what the QR can assert about a purchase, for an OCR result to be checked
against:

- **`total` and `date` are exact** and outrank anything OCR produced for the same receipt.
- **`liters`, `unitPrice` and `fuelKind` are EMPTY - always.** The QR does not carry them. Do
  not derive, guess, or default them. An empty field the user fills is correct; a fabricated
  one is the defect (hard rule 13: every derived value is a default input, never a fact, and
  stays editable at capture and afterwards).
- Must work with **no network at all** (hard rule 1).

### 2b. The cross-check result – the reason this task is worth doing

Given a QR total and a candidate total extracted from the same receipt, classify the outcome.
Return a typed result, do not just return a Bool:

- **agrees** - within a small money tolerance. The OCR total is confirmed.
- **disagrees** - the QR wins for the *grand total*. On the corpus this catches an OCR total
  that was actually the VAT line or the rounding line.
- **suggestsMixedReceipt** - the OCR fuel line is **less than** the QR grand total by more than
  tolerance. That is not an error: it is the signature of a receipt with non-fuel items on it,
  and hard rule 4 says the fill-up amount is the fuel line, **not** the grand total. So the
  fuel line stands and the difference is what a later task offers as separate expenses.

`receipt-009` is the fixture for the third case: QR `s=6264.00`, fuel line `6135.24`, and the
`129.00` difference is a bottle of water - whose price is also, adversarially, the exact same
number as the fuel's price per litre.

**Provide this as a pure function returning a typed result. Do not wire it into the parser, the
duplicate detector or any screen** - those are P2.2/P2.3/P2.4.

### 3. Duplicate identity

`fn` + `i` + `fp` together identify a fiscal document uniquely. Expose a stable identity value
so a re-scan of the same receipt can be recognised as the same purchase rather than a second
fill-up. **Do not wire it into duplicate detection** - just provide the identity and test it.

### 4. Logging

Follow `ios/Sources/TankbookCore/Logging/LogEvents.swift`. **Hard rule 12 is absolute here**:
you may log that a payload parsed, and a failure *reason*, and field *names*. You may **never**
log the total, the timestamp, or any of `fn`/`i`/`fp` - those are the user's purchase.

### Vacuous-assertion traps for this task

- `#expect(true)`, or asserting only that parsing did not throw.
- Testing with invented payloads while the 11 real ones sit unused.
