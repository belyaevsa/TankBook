## The invariant this task exists for - it is P3's exit gate

`docs/SCHEMA.md`, verbatim, on `Expense`:

> `installedInServiceId: UUID?` - `.parts` only: set when a later ServiceRecord installs it.
> **Cost counts ONCE (here, at purchase); the service links it via `usedParts` instead of
> re-pricing it - no double counting in cost/km.**

And `docs/PHASES.md` makes it the phase's exit gate: *"cost/km never double-counts a linked part

So the load-bearing rule is: **linking a part to a service must not change any number.** A part
bought for 12.40 € and later installed contributes 12.40 € to cost/km whether or not it is linked
- the link is provenance, never a price. Get this wrong and every stat silently inflates, which is
precisely the complaint `docs/COMPETITORS.md` records against Drivvo ("wrong economy math").

## What to build

1. **Expense entry**: category, title, money, date - reachable from the same Capture "Service &
   expenses" surface P3.1a built, as a peer of the service path (hard rule 15). `.parts` is an
   ordinary category here; there is no separate "buy a part" flow.
2. **The parts shelf**: `.parts` expenses with `installedInServiceId == nil` are "on shelf". A
   list of them, with what J7b calls a visible **"on shelf" state** - the journey warns that a
   *silent* shelf means forgotten parts.
3. **Install linking**: while editing a ServiceRecord, the Link row offers shelf parts ("Install
   oil filter from Mar 3?"). Accepting sets `installedInServiceId` on the Expense **and** appends
   its id to `usedParts` on the ServiceRecord. **Both sides, one action, atomically** - a
   half-written link is a part that is neither on the shelf nor in the service.
4. **Suggestion matching**: a service whose category is `.oil` offers oil parts first. Keep it
   simple and explainable - the user picks; the app never links automatically (hard rule 13).
5. **Unlinking** returns the part to the shelf and leaves its cost untouched.
6. **Strings** through the String Catalog, EN **and** RU. **Read the rule at the top of
   `ios/App/Sources/Localization/L10n.swift` first** - `Text(_: String)` does not localise, so an
   expression mixing runtime data and copy renders its English key while the gate stays green.
   Five bugs have shipped that way. "Install oil filter from Mar 3?" is exactly that shape.

1. **The double-count property test** (L1) - this is P3's exit gate, so write it as a property
   over compositions, not one example: for a set of fills, services and part expenses, **cost/km
   is identical with and without the link**. Link, unlink, relink; the number must not move.
2. **Both sides of the link are written** (L1): after linking, the Expense carries
   `installedInServiceId` and the ServiceRecord's `usedParts` contains its id. After unlinking,
   neither does.
3. **A linked part still counts once, at purchase** (L1): assert the *amount*, not just that
   nothing crashed - the whole bug class is a number that changes.
4. **Shelf membership** (L1): `.parts` with no `installedInServiceId` are on the shelf; installed
   ones are not; a **deleted** service returns its parts to the shelf rather than stranding them.
5. **L4**: the shelf renders with its "on shelf" state; the Link row offers a shelf part and the
   service shows it after linking; the ordinary service sheet is unchanged when the shelf is empty.
6. **Localization gate** exit 0, RU 100%.

### Vacuous-assertion traps for this task

- `#expect(true)`, or asserting only that a call did not throw.
- A "double count" test with **one** entry - it cannot distinguish counted-once from counted-twice
  if the link changes nothing in that arrangement. Use several entries and compare a computed
  cost/km before and after linking.
- Asserting the link by re-reading the in-memory object you just mutated instead of going through
  the repository.
- Writing only one side of the link and asserting only that side.
- Comparing money as `Double` or within a tolerance. `Money.amount` is `Decimal`; use `Decimal`
  literals.
