## Why this task exists

P4.12 measured the cloud vision model against the rules parser on the same corpus:

| class | rules | DeepSeek VLM | median / max latency |
|---|---|---|---|
| receipts | 46/96 | 84/96 | 7.4 s / 21.8 s |
| pump | 1/46 | 31/46 | 8.3 s / 40.1 s |
| screenshots | 7/24 | 22/24 | 6.5 s / 9.8 s |

Far more accurate everywhere - but with **four problems**: failures that are **silent** (swaps and
scale shifts that pass the arithmetic cross-check), output that is **stochastic** (`pump-009`
flipped between correct and shifted across runs), **per-call cost**, and **every median above the
3 s device budget** `API.md` calls normative.

Self-hosted PaddleOCR attacks three of the four. **Russian is supported**
(`cyrillic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec`; PaddleOCR-VL covers 109 languages), which is the exact gap that
killed the Foundation Models tier.

## Two arms, because together they answer what neither answers alone

**Arm A - PP-OCRv5 Cyrillic recognition -> the EXISTING `FuelExtractor`.**
Isolates *recognition*. This is the arm that matters intellectually, because **it can falsify
`docs/EXTRACTION.md`**. That doc's central claim is that interpretation, not recognition, is the
bottleneck - resting on Vision reading receipts at confidence 1.00 while the parser still misses
half the fields. Feeding a *different reader* into the *same parser* tests it directly:

- Arm A scores about the same as Vision + parser -> the claim is **confirmed**.
- Arm A scores far better -> the claim is **wrong**, and you must **correct
  `docs/EXTRACTION.md` in the same change**. Do not explain the result away.

**Arm B - PaddleOCR-VL -> fields directly.** Competes with the DeepSeek arm on accuracy, latency,
cost and determinism.

## THE TRAP THAT WOULD INVALIDATE ARM A – read this before writing any conversion

`ios/Sources/TankbookCore/Extraction/OCRLine.swift`, verbatim:

> The bounding box uses **Vision's normalized coordinate space (origin bottom-left)**, so a higher
> `midY` sits **higher** on the page.

and `VisionTextRecognizer` sorts lines by **descending `midY`** to read top-to-bottom.

**PaddleOCR returns pixel coordinates with a top-left origin, y increasing downward.** A naive
conversion **inverts the page**, and the parser's geometric rules then fail silently:

- `loneMarkers` finds the price "directly below its `/L` label" via `line.midY - other.midY > 0` -
  inverted, it reads the row's **sum** instead;
- the labelled-column rule compares `midX` against a header's `midX`;
- `OperandPair.first(in:)` takes the **first** operand line in document order, which depends
  entirely on the sort.

So a wrong conversion makes Arm A score badly **for reasons that have nothing to do with
recognition quality** - and would produce a **false confirmation** of the doc's claim. That is the
dangerous direction: it would look like evidence.

**Required:** normalise to Vision's convention - `x/width`, and **flip y** so that
`y_norm = 1 - (y_px / height)` with the rect's origin at its *bottom* edge - then sort descending
by `midY`.

**And prove it with a calibration test before trusting any sweep number.** `receipt-001` is a
fixture the rules parser reads **exactly** (liters, unit price and total all correct, cross-check
locks at `67.00 x 1.869 = 125.22`). Run Arm A over it and assert the parser returns those same
fields. **If that test fails, your coordinate conversion is wrong and every other number in this
task is meaningless.** Write it first.

6. **Variance**: where the three runs disagree for an image, that is recorded and asserted, not
   averaged away.

### Vacuous-assertion traps for this task

- Scoring an arm with a different tolerance than the rules arm it is being compared to.
- Running each image once and calling the model deterministic.
- Reporting only aggregate percentages. **Two silent failures matter more than a higher average** -
  that is the whole lesson of P4.12.
- Trusting a sweep whose calibration test never ran, or ran and failed.
- Treating a failed call as a skip, which raises the score by losing data.
- Concluding "recognition doesn't help" from an Arm A run whose coordinate conversion was never
  verified.
