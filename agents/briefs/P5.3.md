## The rule this task exists to serve, and the two times it has already shipped broken

`CLAUDE.md` hard rule 10: all user-facing strings go through String Catalogs, EN + RU from day one.
That is **met**. What is not met is correctness *inside* the Russian strings.

**Instance 1 (P1.4).** `"%@ spend"` was translated `"%@ расходы"` and rendered
**"АВГУСТ РАСХОДЫ"** - word-order nonsense. The rule written then: a composed string needs a full
localised phrase per language, never concatenation.

**Instance 2 (P4.7).** That rule did **not** prevent the second one, because this *was* a full
phrase: `"from your %1$@, %2$@"` → `"с вашего %1$@, %2$@"` rendered **"с вашего телефон Android"**.
`с вашего` governs the genitive; `%1$@` receives a **server-supplied device name** that cannot be
declined. No better translation fixes it - the sentence shape is wrong.

**So the sharper rule, and the core of this task:**

> **If a `%@` receives runtime data, the surrounding phrase must not govern its case.**

Both were caught only by a human reading rendered Russian. No test sees them. This task is where
they get found on purpose instead of by luck.

## The concrete work list, already measured

`ios/App/Sources/Localizable.xcstrings` holds **42 EN keys with a `%@` inside a multi-word
phrase** - the exact risk class. A sample:

```
%@ already recorded %d km.
%@ came back with 1 new entry – stays archived.
Changed by sync · %@, %@
Completed today at %1$@ km
Consumption updated: %1$@ → %2$@ %3$@
Due at %1$@ km or %2$@
Archived · %1$@ · history kept
In %1$@ km
```

**Audit every one of the 42.** For each, answer two questions and act:

1. **What does the slot receive?** A *number or a date* formatted by the app is usually safe. A
   **station name, device name, car name, make/model, fuel grade, or any user- or server-supplied
   text** is not - it arrives in the nominative and cannot be declined.
2. **Does the Russian phrase govern that slot's case?** A preposition (`с`, `в`, `на`, `от`, `до`,
   `у`), a verb with an object, or an adjective agreeing with it - all govern. A slot after a
   separator (`·`, `,`, `→`), at the start of a sentence, or in a nominative apposition does not.

Where both are true, **change the sentence shape** - do not hunt for a cleverer translation. The
usual fixes: put the slot in the nominative at the head (`«Volvo V60 — архив»`), move it behind a
separator, or restructure so no preposition reaches it. **State the shape change in your report,
not just the new string.**

Record the audit as a table in `docs/VISION.md`'s localization section (or a new
`docs/LOCALIZATION.md` if it wants one - say which you chose and why): key, what the slot receives,
governed or not, and the resolution. **A future translator needs the reasoning, not just the
result.**

## The plural edges the current tests do not reach

19 keys carry plural variations and **all 19 already have complete RU categories**
(`one`/`few`/`many`/`other`) - so "add the missing forms" is not the work either. The work is that
**Russian plural selection is not 1/2/5**:

| n | RU category | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 21, 31, 101 | `one` | 1 запись, **21 запись** |
| 2-4, 22-24 | `few` | 2 записи, **22 записи** |
| 5-20, 25-30, **11-14** | `many` | 5 записей, **11 записей**, **111 записей** |

**11-14 are the trap**: they end in 1-4 but take `many`, so a test of 1/2/5 passes an
implementation that gets 11 and 21 wrong. Assert **1, 2, 5, 11, 21** for the plural keys that can
realistically reach those values (entry counts, day counts, car counts, receipt items).

`docs/TASKS.md` names `1 литр / 2 литра / 5 литров` for this row. **Check whether the app ever
renders the word `литр` at all** - the UI uses the abbreviation `л`, which does not decline. If no
surface renders the spelled-out unit, **say so and do not invent a plural nobody displays**;
correct the TASKS row in the same change. Inventing an unrendered plural to satisfy a checklist is
worse than an honest note.

## The blind spot a green gate cannot see

At the top of `ios/App/Sources/Localization/L10n.swift`, four instances are recorded of this shape:

> **`Text(_: String)` does not localise.** Any expression producing a `String` renders its English
> key even when the catalogue has the translation - and the gate **cannot see it**, because the key
> *is* present.

The shape is always **runtime data and copy sharing one expression**. Sweep `ios/App/Sources` for
it and fix what you find; if the localization gate can be taught to catch the shape (a `Text(` whose
argument is an interpolation or a `String` variable rather than a literal), **extend the gate** -
that is worth more than the individual fixes. If it cannot be caught statically, say why.

### Vacuous-assertion traps named for this task

- Plural tests at 1/2/5 only - **11 and 21 are the whole point**.
- Asserting a key exists in the catalogue rather than asserting what it **renders**.
- "Fixing" a governed phrase by translating it differently while keeping the shape - the P4.7
  lesson is that no translation fixes a wrong sentence shape.
- Adding a `литр` plural that no screen renders, to tick the row.
- `#expect(true)`, or asserting only that a view builds.
