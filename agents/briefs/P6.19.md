## The defect, already measured

Computed from `design/tokens.json` and verified twice:

| Text | Fill | dark | light |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Color.white` | `warn` | **2.15:1** | 5.23 |
| `Color.white` | `taillight` | **3.47:1** | 5.07 |
| `Theme.Palette.midnight` | `warn` | **8.66:1** | 4.84 |
| `Theme.Palette.midnight` | `taillight` | **5.37:1** | 4.69 |

2.15:1 is below even the 3:1 large-text floor, in the **dark theme, which is the brand's home**.
3.47:1 is every primary button in the app. `midnight` clears 4.5:1 on both fills in **both** themes,
so one substitution fixes it - and it adapts, because `midnight` is near-black in dark and near-white
in light.

1. Replace the white foreground with the token that clears AA on that fill in both themes.
2. **Extend the contrast guard with a TEXT-ON-ACCENT dimension.** This is the point of the task.
   `PaletteAccentGuardTests.accentContrastClearsAA` currently checks accent-on-BACKGROUND only. That
   narrowness is why this defect survived two rounds of contrast work today: the first version
   checked two accents, the widened version checked four, and **neither ever checked text drawn on
   an accent fill**. Add the missing dimension, and enumerate the class rather than the case in
   front of you.

   fill in `ios/App/Sources`. A text scan is acceptable - say plainly in the test what it cannot see.
