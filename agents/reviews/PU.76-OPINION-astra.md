# PU.76 — independent method opinion

2026-09-24. Read-only review by Codex astra. I did not read `agents/research/PU.76.md`, contact its author, train a model, or run a build. The experiments below are proposals, not results.

**Spike a small pretrained one-stage oriented detector first, preceded by a cheap representation/oracle check.** Prefer a rotated-rectangle head if that check says rectangles preserve the hand-quad reading advantage; otherwise use a lightweight direct-quadrilateral head in the TextBoxes++ family. Keep the existing Hough deskew as the control. Do not spend the first training budget porting a two-stage research detector.

## What the repository actually establishes

`PumpRowDetector.swift` already exposes four corners, but manufactures them from `VNRecognizedObjectObservation.boundingBox`. The public row representation is suitable; the learned output and its Vision adapter are the limiting seam. A replacement must emit learned geometry, not put another four-corner wrapper around the same upright observation.

PU.76 records 89 commits at 0.944 precision for hand upright bounds versus 111 at 0.991 for hand quads. This is evidence for better **geometry**, not proof that predicting angle alone recovers 22 commits: a hand quad also supplies endpoints, height and perspective. The current EXTRACTION apportionment paragraph instead reports 108 for its oracle arm, with 33 cells lost at framing. These snapshots must not be combined into a synthetic benchmark. Freeze annotations, scorer and reader revision and rerun all arms together before judging a candidate. “Cells” here are scored field values, not individual digit glyphs.

PU.66's hand-only round improved on the tracker-contaminated round, but still lost to the shipped detector downstream. That supports consistent hand labels and task-specific scoring; it does not prove oriented regression cannot work on this corpus. PU.76 itself records hand-label agreement around IoU 0.75–0.79, uncomfortably close to the tight-box gate. Resolve the annotation rule before architecture comparisons: include the complete reading window, decimal mark and intended blank positions consistently; do not alternate between visible ink and bezel.

PU.70's concrete pump-302 observation matters more than its general geometric explanation: the detector spans 0.10 of the frame where the hand row spans 0.30. Turning that fragment cannot discover its missing ends. Also, the task's assertion `L cos θ + H sin θ >= L` is not universally true. For positive angles it holds only while `tan(θ/2) <= H/L`. This wording error does not invalidate the measured threefold under-framing.

## Ranking for this corpus and device

Ranks reflect expected usefulness as the eventual detector, not the order of every diagnostic. Payoffs are engineering expectations, not measured gains.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Small pretrained one-stage OBB detector; lightweight TextBoxes++-style quad head if needed** | Learns the entire row extent and orientation together, so can address both loose upright envelopes and truncated detections. One class, one feature extractor, and a compact head are a plausible replacement for the 31 MB model. Direct quad regression can retain perspective when a five-parameter rotated rectangle cannot. TextBoxes++ establishes the single-shot oriented-text approach [1]; using a small modern backbone is an adaptation, not a reproduction. | A few hundred independent photos may not teach precise endpoints; augmentation does not add independent scenes. Generic or aerial pretraining is not evidence of pump-domain accuracy. Thin rows can disappear at excessive feature stride. Angle periodicity, width/height swaps and corner order can silently break crops. Standard tensor inference is relatively approachable; polygon decode/suppression and preprocessing parity remain integration work. Set a provisional **<=31 MB shipped detector** budget; do not assume a “nano” name guarantees it. | First replace every hand quad with its pixel-space minimum-area rectangle and compare reader output with the original quad. If that loses much of the advantage, reject the OBB representation and retain this rank only as a quad-head variant. Next export an unmodified small pretrained candidate before fine-tuning and compare its tensors with the source model. A bounded hand-only training pilot must beat the upright/Hough control on a train-internal validation split in tight polygon IoU and correct commits. If it cannot learn precise training boxes either, investigate labels/resolution before another long run. |
| **2. Lightweight row-region segmentation → components → minimum-area rectangle; PixelLink-style links if rows merge** | Hand quads can directly supply filled row-region masks and instance/link targets without new pixel annotation. Dense supervision may be easier than coordinate regression on scarce images. It can find full extents independently of the old detector. PixelLink provides the instance-linking precedent [2]. | Many pixels from one still are correlated, not thousands of independent examples. Target the whole annotated row region, not seven disconnected digit strokes. Neighbouring rows may merge; reflections and bezel edges may distort the component. A coarse mask shifts a thin row boundary substantially, and a minimum-area rectangle discards perspective. High-resolution activations and CPU component/link decoding can dominate latency despite small weights. | Before training, rasterize hand quads at the proposed output stride, reconstruct components/rectangles, and run the reader. If even these perfect masks lose the required cells or merge rows, reject that resolution/representation. Only then train a tiny segmentation pilot. If it predicts good masks but gives bad crops, more network training will not fix the geometry decoder. Add links only if simple components demonstrably merge rows. |
| **3. Keep upright detector + existing per-row Hough turn** | Almost no extra model bytes or training; likely helps already complete, slightly turned boxes. Cheapest control and possible bounded improvement. | Primarily a geometry ceiling, not conversion risk. In `deskew`, search padding expands height, not width; the ordinary path preserves centre and, at small angles, dimensions. `rowSize` assumes the upright box bounds the full row. None repairs the pump-302 fragment. Hough confidence is not calibrated probability that a crop is correct. Runtime grows with candidate count and retries. | On matched detector rows compare unchanged boxes, current FHT turn, and **oracle hand angle applied to the same detector box with the same sizing rule**. If even the oracle-angle arm leaves the framing loss above the target, angle estimation cannot solve PU.76. Include misses as failures and the pump-302 subset. This is a ceiling test, not a deployable use of labels. |
| **4. Two-stage oriented proposals: Oriented R-CNN or Ma et al.'s RRPN** | Oriented proposals followed by refinement offer an explicit mechanism for fitting long rows; useful as an offline reference if simpler heads plateau. Oriented R-CNN simplifies oriented proposal generation [4]; RRPN introduces rotation proposals and rotated ROI pooling [3]. | Highest deployment effort here: rotated ROI operations, proposal filtering and suppression must survive conversion or be reimplemented. Published backbone configurations and GPU throughput do not establish iPhone 12 size or latency. More machinery does not remove the hand-label ceiling. | Before any corpus training, export a representative forward path including rotated ROI extraction and verify numerical parity, compiled size and iPhone 12 execution. Stop if meeting the budget requires replacing its defining operators or retaining a large CPU stage. A desktop accuracy run alone would not falsify the deployment concern. |

## The first spike, concretely

Run the representation check and Hough ceiling control first; they are cheaper than any retrain and decide whether rank 1 needs OBBs or true quads. The first **learned** spike is rank 1. Start from a small pretrained OBB implementation with a documented Core ML export path; pin the exact version and output convention. The current Ultralytics OBB documentation lists Core ML export, but its four-point label format does not imply an unrestricted perspective-quad head. Its internal rectangle/angle representation is explicit. This is an implementation candidate, not a paper citation or a claim that its export was tested here. [Official OBB documentation](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/obb/).

Keep the exported graph to fixed-shape tensor inference initially. Decode to the existing TL/TR/BR/BL, top-left-origin contract in Swift; explicitly invert image scaling/padding, and do geometry in pixels before normalizing. Use the existing upright orientation search to establish reading direction: an OBB axis alone does not distinguish 0 from 180 degrees. Bound proposal count before polygon NMS if the selected head requires NMS. Do not assume Vision's current object-observation adapter or ordinary upright NMS preserves oriented geometry. Apple's documented PyTorch capture/conversion workflow supports this route, but actual operator support and numerical parity must be checked on the selected graph. [Apple conversion workflow](https://apple.github.io/coremltools/docs-guides/source/convert-pytorch-workflow.html). The exporter also distinguishes raw outputs from embedded NMS; its documented embedded-NMS task list is not a promise of rotated NMS. [Exporter documentation](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml/).

Use only hand-verified windows for the first comparison, identical source photos and augmentations across candidates. Split train/validation by capture/session or pump, keeping adjacent video frames together. Fit thresholds and select checkpoints on train-internal validation; leave the established heldout split untouched until selection. Rotate pixels and quads together. Sample small turns densely because they dominate the recorded data, retaining a controlled larger-turn tail. Report pixel-space angle bins: PU.65's earlier normalized-coordinate angles were corrected in PU.69, so “past 12 degrees” is not a reliable physical-angle bin without recomputation.

Before training, record the acceptance rule in rotated/polygon IoU: median IoU and recall@0.7 must hold or improve and false rows/photo must not rise, under the same one-to-one matching. Also report complete-row coverage/end clipping and verifier kept/dropped/missed counts. IoU alone can conceal a clipped decimal or endpoint; the unchanged downstream reader is decisive.

Reproduce PU.76's framing loss below roughly 15 cells and live count around 65 with zero wrong readings, reporting **committed / correct / wrong / total** separately for `readPhoto` and `PumpDisplayCapture.classify`. Keep thresholds, classifier and reading law fixed. Include non-pump fixtures, level rows, the large-turn subset and pump-275. These are the task's targets, not this opinion's predictions; a single failed pilot rejects that candidate/configuration, not mathematically every member of its family. The 111/0.991 oracle snapshot is not an attainable guaranteed live floor, nor evidence of zero errors.

Measure compiled/shipped bytes and physical iPhone 12 Release cold-load time, warm p50/p95 detector-plus-decode time, peak memory and full refusal-path latency against the shipped detector. No absolute latency budget is supplied, so use non-regression as the initial criterion and state any tradeoff. EXTRACTION's 2.9–3.1 ms per-row FHT figure is a repository measurement, not a verified iPhone 12 result. Check FP16 first; evaluate compression separately so precision loss is not confused with architecture failure.

## Verified paper identities

All four exist: I opened their primary arXiv records on 2026-09-24 and verified title, author list and year/venue metadata. This verifies identities and the stated architectural families; it is not reproduction of their results or validation of an iOS port.

1. Minghui Liao, Baoguang Shi, Xiang Bai. **TextBoxes++: A Single-Shot Oriented Scene Text Detector** (2018), IEEE Transactions on Image Processing 27, 3676–3690. [arXiv:1801.02765](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02765). Verified. This is the oriented paper, not the earlier TextBoxes paper.
2. Dan Deng, Haifeng Liu, Xuelong Li, Deng Cai. **PixelLink: Detecting Scene Text via Instance Segmentation** (2018), AAAI. [arXiv:1801.01315](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01315). Verified. Its reported data efficiency motivates a test; it does not establish sufficiency of this pump corpus.
3. Jianqi Ma, Weiyuan Shao, Hao Ye, Li Wang, Hong Wang, Yingbin Zheng, Xiangyang Xue. **Arbitrary-Oriented Scene Text Detection via Rotation Proposals** (2018), IEEE Transactions on Multimedia 20(11), 3111–3122; preprint first submitted 2017. [arXiv:1703.01086](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01086). Verified. PU.76's “TPAMI 2018” venue is incorrect. This is also distinct from the similarly named rotation-sensitive regression paper.
4. Xingxing Xie, Gong Cheng, Jiabao Wang, Xiwen Yao, Junwei Han. **Oriented R-CNN for Object Detection** (2021), ICCV. [arXiv:2108.05699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05699). Verified. Its published desktop GPU benchmark does not estimate phone performance.
